# GELS V3.2 — Deformable cuboidal granules, compaction past close packing, asynchronous cell cycle

Status: approved 2026-09-18. Phases 0-10 landed; see `CodeLog/Updates/CHANGELOG.md` under
`[V3.2]` for what each one actually measured. The preceding plan is `CodeLog/ClaudesPlan/3.1.md`.

**Landed** (280 tests green, V2.7 fixtures bit-identical): the whole of Part C (cell cycle),
the whole of Part B (soft contact) including B5, and the core of Part A -- the closed-form
directional radius and its gradient, the corrected bounding radius, the RSA deflation fix, the
shape-aware settle with rotation, the shape-aware overlap projection / wall clamp / bed
surface, and the curvature cap. Five V3.1 bugs fixed along the way (division never fired; the
parent reset landed on the wrong cell after the CSR rebuild; `r_bound` did not bound a blocky
granule; RSA dropped granules silently; the elastic rail was overwritten on the config path).

**Corrections to this plan, found by measurement rather than assumed:**
- A1's force formula is wrong as written. `lambda` is homogeneous of degree **-1**, so
  `grad(lambda).u = -lambda`, not 0, and the radial part must be projected out. Verified
  against finite differences: with the projection the gradient matches `d(gap)/dx` to 2e-10;
  without it the error is 1.37 -- a factor of ~2.5 and a non-conservative force.
- The bounding-sphere volume ratio at AR 1.8 / n 3.5 is **2.39x**, not 1.97x.
- The flat-face curvature blow-up is ~1e15 um, not 328 -- a force factor of ~1e6, not 26.
- The 2 % RSA-drop hard error is too aggressive: small dense systems legitimately lose a few
  granules, so it is a warning.
- `shape_margin` is worse than "blunt": it breaks post-relax convergence without helping.

**Blocker found after the plan was written, and it outranks everything below.**
`se2d_contact_k` -- the common-normal solver the DYNAMICS uses for shaped granules, unchanged
since V1.3 -- does not return a penetration depth. `delta` is the distance between the two
common-normal surface points. Measured against the exact support-function MTD: at n = 3.5 it
**misses every contact**; at n = 2.0 it reports 34.7 um where the truth is 0.73 um. On a
packed bed its mean reported overlap is 889x the settle's. Those phantom overlaps are why a
shaped bed expands 12.3 % over 72 h with no cells. The settle's directional-radius proxy is
NOT at fault -- it tracks the true MTD to within 3 % at n = 3.5, so A3's premise ("the proxy
is permissive") was wrong, and its remedy (a certificate, or promoting the exact contact into
the SETTLE) fixes the wrong half. What is needed is the support-function MTD in the
**dynamics**, replacing `se2d_contact_k` / `se3d_contact_k`'s delta, normal and contact point.
It is closed form for n1 == n2, exact, and validated here against a brute-force minimisation.
Nothing about shaped-granule physics should be trusted until it lands.

**Not built** (Part A remainder, in priority order): the reference twin of the shape-aware
settle (so `test_packing_vs_reference` cannot yet gate the flag on); the support-function
certificate of A3 and its promotion into the settle, which is what would close the residual
~4.6 % bed relaxation; wall torque and the `omega x r_c` term; rolling resistance; per-granule
sphere dispatch; the blockiness-aware RCP formula and the viz de-duplication; the
`se3d_contact_k` allocation-free rewrite; and the `--sweep compaction` condition set.

## Context

V3.1 closed the geometry and force-balance gaps — a well with a free top, gravity, a sedimenting
packer, a Hill contractile bridge, cell division — and ended with an honest bound: **rigid
monodisperse spheres cannot pack past φ ≈ 0.64, so densification alone lowers a bed by at most
7–15 %.** The user's response identifies why that bound does not apply to their experiment:

1. **The granules are not spheres.** They are irregular, roughly cuboidal fragments with an aspect
   ratio and surface roughness. Random packings of blocky superellipsoids jam at φ ≈ 0.72–0.74
   (Delaney & Cleary 2010), so the ceiling moves from 0.64 to ~0.70.
2. **The granules are not rigid.** They are hydrogel at **10–50 kPa**, not the 3 GPa PMMA V3.1 was
   built around, so contacts genuinely flatten — and cell traction across a contact tightens it.
3. **The cell population is synchronised.** Every cell starts at cycle phase zero, so divisions
   arrive in a burst rather than spread through the population.

User decisions taken for this plan: soft gel **replaces** PMMA as the primary system; granules are
**irregular fragments** with aspect ratio and blockiness sampled per granule; the experimental
compaction target is **not yet quantified**, so the plan builds the knobs and brackets the range;
the cell fix goes as far as a **proper cell cycle** (random starting phase *and* a per-cell cycle
length), not just a jittered start.

### What the audit found

Three findings reshape the work. All were measured against the current code, not assumed.

**The shape path is not merely missing — it is backwards.** The packer is bounding-sphere
throughout: `RSAChecker` and `settle_sub_k` (`gels/kernels/packing.py:111,196`) use only
`r_bound`, and `p.shape_enabled` is stored at `packing.py:120` and never read. `r_bound =
max(a,b,c)` (`engine.py:481`) ignores blockiness, so for `n > 2` it does not even bound the body.
With semi-axes renormalised to equal volume (`engine.py:2424,2607`), an AR-1.8 / n-3.5 fragment's
bounding sphere claims **1.97×** the granule's volume. Turning shapes on today therefore jams the
packer at a *true* solid fraction near 0.33. Orientations are drawn once and frozen, and wall
contacts emit no torque, so a fragment can never rotate to nest or flop flat on the floor —
exactly the mechanism the density gain depends on.

**The deformation path already exists at default settings and the V3.1 preset removed it.**
`_resolve_overlaps` permits a standing overlap of `max_overlap_frac · r` for free and projects away
only the excess. The volumetric multiplier is `(2/(2−mof))³`: at the **default 0.15** that is
**1.263**, i.e. the engine already admits ~26 % densification past touching (φ 0.60 → 0.758). The
`pmma_well` preset sets `0.03` → 1.046, which is what capped the V3.1 well runs. Separately, at
10 kPa / r = 40 µm a 180 nN bridge equilibrates at δ = 2.85 µm (7.1 % of r), comfortably inside the
default ceiling. **Soft-contact compaction is mostly a parameter and metric problem, not a new
solver** — which makes it far cheaper than the shape work and probably a larger effect.

**V3.1's cell division never fires.** `gs.cell_age` is allocated, read by `divide_cells` and reset
on division, but **nothing increments it anywhere**. The compiled kernel parameter named `cell_age`
(`gels/kernels/cells.py:241`) is bound to `gs.cell_bridge_age` at the call site (`cells.py:803`) —
a name collision that hid it. With the shipped default `min_age_h = 8.0`, which
`fibroblast_realistic` uses, `cell_age >= 8.0` is false forever and **zero cells divide in any real
run**. The suite stayed green because every division test passes `min_age = 0.0`.

### Two limits to keep in view

- **The reported φ will be wrong before it is fixed.** `phi_solid_true` and `phi_bed`
  (`engine.py:3324,3419`) sum `(4/3)πr³` over nominal radii and double-count every overlap lens.
  Compaction *through* overlap is precisely the regime where that error is largest.
- **Every load-bearing contact is outside the explicit integrator's stability window,** even at
  10 kPa: the bound is `k < γ/dt` = 2–4 nN/µm, crossed at δ ≈ 0.01 µm. Non-penetration is enforced
  by the overlap projection and the velocity cap, not by resolved forces. V3.2 must either fix this
  or restate it precisely; it must not quietly rely on it.

## Part A — Irregular cuboidal granules (problem 1)

Target: a gravity-settled bed of AR ~ N(1.8, 0.4), blockiness n ~ N(3.5, 1.0) fragments reaches
**φ_bed 0.66–0.72**, against a sphere control of 0.55–0.60.

### A1. The shape-aware contact proxy (the core)

For the GELS implicit function `F = (|x/a|^n1 + |y/b|^n1)^(n2/n1) + |z/c|^n2` (`engine.py:1313`),
`F` is positively homogeneous of degree `n2` for **any** `(n1, n2)`, so the directional radius is
closed form:

```
λ(u) = superellipsoid_implicit(u_x, u_y, u_z, a, b, c, n1, n2) ** (-1/n2)      # 3D
λ(u) = (|u_x/a|^n + |u_y/b|^n) ** (-1/n)                                        # 2D
```

Verified to 2e-15 over 2000 random directions at five `(n1,n2)` pairs. No Newton fallback needed.

**A radial force alone does not nest cuboids** — the torque arm is parallel to the force, so the
torque is identically zero. The pair overlap must be treated as a scalar gap whose *gradient*
supplies both force and torque. With `g = λ_i(u) + λ_j(−u) − d`, `κ = k_rep·g^{3/2}` (same
magnitude as today's `k_rep·ov^1.5`) and `G = R ∇Λ(Rᵀu)`:

```
F_j = κ ( u − (G_i − G_j)/d )      F_i = −F_j
τ_i = κ ( u × G_i )                τ_j = −κ ( u × G_j )
```

Properties worth unit-testing directly: it is a true potential (`E = (2/5) k_rep g^{5/2}`), so the
settle is gradient descent and cannot limit-cycle; linear and angular momentum are conserved
identically; and for two parallel cuboids it recovers the exact face normal, which is why it nests.
`∇Λ·b = 0` exactly by Euler homogeneity — a free assertion.

Measured convergence at settle tolerance 0.05·mean_r, 500 granules, AR 1.8 / n 3.5:

| target φ | gradient tilt + rotation | no rotation | radial force only |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.64 | converged | converged | fails |
| 0.68 | **converged** | fails | fails |
| 0.70 | **converged** | fails | fails |
| 0.74 | **converged** | fails | fails |

**The proxy is permissive and must not be the number we publish.** It is provably a lower bound on
the true touching distance (`λ_i + λ_j ≤ r_K(u)`, so never a phantom repulsion), measured at
mean 0.939 / min 0.80 of exact. At a proxy φ of 0.70 the true minimum translation distance is
mean 0.13 r_eq, **p95 0.32 r_eq** — against the 0.05 r_eq the sphere path enforces. Hence A3.

Keep the exact common-normal solver (`se2d/se3d_contact_k`) in the **dynamics**, where `R_eff` and
a defensible contact area are needed; the proxy is for the **packer** and the overlap projection.

### A2. Correct `r_bound`, and why it must ship with A1

For `n1 == n2 == n > 2`, maximising `r² = Σ s_k² u_k^{2/n}` on the simplex gives

```
r_bound = s_max · ( Σ_k (s_k/s_max)^q )^(1/2 − 1/n),     q = 2n/(n−2)
```

(the ratio form avoids overflow as `n → 2⁺`; fall back to `s_max` for `n ≤ 2 + 1e-6` and for
`n < 2`). Isotropic check: `s·3^(1/2−1/n)`. For `n1 ≠ n2` use a 20-step golden section over the
`xy`/`z` split — it runs once per granule per inflation step in numpy, never in a kernel.

| shape (equal-volume) | `max(a,b,c)` | true `r_bound` | `(r_bound/r_eq)³` |
|---|---|---|---|
| sphere | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.00 |
| AR 1.0, n 3.5 | 0.880 | **1.114** | 1.38 |
| AR 1.8, n 3.5 | 1.181 | **1.253** | **1.97** |

**Fixing `r_bound` alone makes packing strictly worse** (the bounding-sphere ceiling falls from
0.39 to 0.33 true solid fraction), so it ships behind the same flag as A1, never separately.

Two consequences that must be handled in the same change:

- **RSA saturates.** `packing_placement` (`engine.py:3294`) sets `alpha` so the deflated
  *equal-volume-sphere* fraction is `phi_safe = 0.20`, but RSA tests bounding spheres, giving an
  effective 0.393 at AR 1.8 / n 3.5 — past the 0.384 3D RSA saturation. Granules are then silently
  dropped at `engine.py:2648` (`if not placed: pass`). Fix with a deterministic
  `shape_bound_factor(species, p, dim)` = `E[(r_bound/r_eq)^dim]` from the species *moments* (no
  RNG draws, so the stream is untouched), used as `alpha = (phi_safe/(phi_place·κ))^(1/dim)`.
  Add a hard error when RSA drops more than ~2 % of the target count; the current silent `pass` is
  how this would otherwise surface as a mystery at the φ report.
- **`bed_surface` (`engine.py:3352`) must use `λ(+ẑ)`, not `r_bound`,** or the bed height rises ~6 %
  and `phi_bed` — the headline metric — falls by the same amount.

Other sites: neighbour cutoffs (`packing.py:407`, `contact2d.py:339`, `contact3d.py:324`; +19 %
candidate pairs in 3D, accepted), RSA placement windows, `apply_position_bounds` (`engine.py:3534`,
use `λ(−n̂_w)`), the bridging gap (`bridging.py:59`, `cells.py:308`), and the `_resolve_overlaps`
threshold (A4).

### A3. The certificate — so the published φ is real

For `n1 == n2 == n` the support function is closed form, because the body is `diag(a,b,c)` applied
to the unit Lⁿ ball and the support function of that ball is the Hölder dual norm:

```
h(ν) = ( |a ν_x|^m + |b ν_y|^m + |c ν_z|^m )^(1/m),   m = n/(n−1)
∇h(ν)_k = s_k^m |ν_k|^(m−1) sgn(ν_k) · h^(1−m)        (= the support point)
```

Verified against a brute-force maximum over an 80 000-point mesh: max relative error 3.8e-5. For
`n1 ≠ n2` it is wrong by up to +12 %, so guard on `|n1−n2| < 1e-9`. Superellipsoids are centrally
symmetric, so with `r = x_j − x_i`,

```
overlap ⟺ δ = min_{|ν|=1} [ h_i(ν) + h_j(ν) − ν·r ] > 0
```

and `δ` **is** the minimum translation distance, `ν*` the contact normal, `∇h_i(ν*)` and `−∇h_j(ν*)`
the contact points. (Sign trap: `max_ν [ν·r − h_K(ν)]` is the *separation*, not the penetration.)

Run it once at the end of the settle over contacting pairs (30–50 projected-gradient steps,
~20 µs/pair → **0.8 s at 40 000 contacts**, free) and report
`packing_penetration_mean / _p95 / _max` in units of mean `r_eq`. **Acceptance is gated on these,
not on the proxy overlap.**

`packing.shape_margin` (η, scaling every λ by `1+η`) is exposed but defaults to 0: measured, η = 1.07
drops mean penetration only 7 % while the reachable φ collapses from 0.70 to 0.64. It is a blunt
instrument and the plan says so rather than pretending otherwise.

### A4. Making the packed bed survive the dynamics

At a true φ = 0.68 with AR 1.8 / n 3.5, the *bounding-sphere* φ is 1.34 — so `_resolve_overlaps`
(`integrate.py:44`, `reference.py:2097`) sees every contact as a deep overlap and violently
separates the bed within a few steps. Under `contact.shape_dynamics`, both twins use the same
tier-A proxy (the right bias here: it is a geometric safety net run twice per step):

```
g         = η·(λ_i(u) + λ_j(−u)) − dist
threshold = max_overlap_frac · min(λ_i(u), λ_j(−u))          # locally correct scale
```

Cost ~1–2 ms/step in 3D (already `parallel=True`). The mobile/immobile split and pair-order
accumulation stay verbatim.

### A5. Rotation, walls, and the curvature trap

- **Rotation in the settle.** `settle_sub_k` gains `shape_mode, a, b, c, n1, n2, theta, quat,
  eta_margin, rot_enabled` and returns `(max_ov, max_disp, max_rot)`. The entire existing body stays
  verbatim under `if shape_mode == 0:` — do **not** unify the sphere case through λ, because
  `λ(u)` for a sphere is `r` in exact arithmetic but not bit-for-bit. Add a cheap
  `if d >= rb[i]+rb[j]: continue` pre-reject (4.6 ns, kills ~60 % of candidates). Orientation
  update with `I_s = (a²+b²+c²)/3` (matching `engine.py:3641`), `ω = τ/I_s`,
  `Δθ = ω·min(dt_settle, THETA_CAP/|ω|)`, `THETA_CAP` = 0.12 rad (3D) / 0.15 (2D) as **module
  constants** beside `dt_settle` and `k_rep`, not `Params` fields. Write an allocation-free
  quaternion twin in `packing.py` (`quat_integrate` allocates; 6e7 calls over a 3D pack). Write
  `gs.omega`/`omega_3d` so the packer's output is self-consistent, and add `max_rot·r_gyr` to the
  stop rule so the settle cannot exit while the bed is still re-orienting.
- **Walls.** Replace the settle's hard clamp with the same penalty using `u → −n̂_w` (this is what
  lets a flat fragment actually lie flat on the floor, and `phi_bed` is measured against a
  wall-referenced envelope). In the dynamics, `se2d_wall_k` (`geometry2d.py:113`), `wall3d_k` and
  `wall3d_plane_k` (`geometry3d.py:194,150`) already locate the deepest surface point; extend their
  returns to carry it, then accumulate `τ += r_c × F_w` in `gather_2d`/`gather_3d` and both twins.
- **`ω × r_c` in the contact relative velocity.** `contact2d.py:115`, `contact3d.py:109` use
  `vel[j] − vel[i]` only, so a granule spinning in place feels no frictional resistance. The contact
  point is already computed in the same loop, so the arm is free.
- **Cap `R_eff`.** `superellipsoid_curvature_radii` explodes at flat faces — measured at equal
  volume, AR 1.8: at **n = 3.5** the face-on `R_eff` is **25.3 (+x) and 328 (+z)** against 0.5–0.9
  for a sphere, and `F ∝ √R_eff`, so face contacts are **7–26× too stiff** and the bed cannot hold
  φ = 0.68. New `contact.curvature_R_cap` (default 0 = off, recommended 2.0) clamping
  `R_eff ≤ cap · min(r_i, r_j)` in `pair_pass_2d:95`, `pair_pass_3d:74`, `geometry3d.py:140`. This
  is pre-existing behaviour frozen by fixture `run2d_shapes`, so the cap is opt-in.

### A6. Roughness

State the mechanism plainly, because it is routinely inverted: **shape and rotation are what reach
φ ≈ 0.68–0.70; friction and rolling resistance *lower* φ_J.** So the settle stays frictionless (as
today) and roughness lives in the **dynamics**, where its job is to *hold* a low-coordination blocky
structure against cell-driven rearrangement.

- `contact.friction_mu_roll` (default 0.0, recommended 0.1–0.3 for visibly rough fragments),
  directional-constant model: `τ_r = −µ_r · R_eff · |F_n| · ω̂_rel`, with a per-step cap
  `|τ_r| ≤ I_s|ω_rel|/dt` so the resistance cannot reverse the spin within one step. No new
  per-contact state. Add Ai, Chen, Rotter & Ooi, *Powder Technology* 206, 269 (2011) to
  `REFERENCES.md`, which currently has no rolling-resistance entry.
- **Static friction: the knob already exists, and it is bounded.** `F_t = F_t_cap·tanh(v_t/v_ref)`
  with `friction_v_ref = 1.0 µm/h` mobilises only ~10 % of the Coulomb cap at the 0.1–1 µm/h creep
  speeds of a compacting bed. Lowering `v_ref` makes friction rigid-plastic for free — but the
  explicit stability bound is `v_ref ≳ µF_n/γ`, which at µ = 0.3, F_n = 50 nN, γ = 1 nN·h/µm is
  **15 µm/h**, close to `v_max`. So `friction_v_ref` is the yield-stress knob, it should be swept,
  and a runtime warning should fire when it is set below the bound. A Cundall–Strack tangential
  spring is the only unconditionally stable answer and needs persistent per-contact state that the
  architecture does not carry (`half_pairs` rebuilds every step) — **DEFER**, documented, with the
  design sketched: an open-addressed `(i,j) → (δ_t, age)` map rebuilt on the neighbour cadence.

### A8. Per-granule sphere dispatch (SHOULD)

`gs.is_circle` is one global bool (`engine.py:471`), so a single non-spherical granule routes every
pair through the Newton solver — and `from_snapshot_dict:641` sets it False unconditionally in 3D.
This bites exactly the configuration we run: `boundary_layer_enabled` places perfect spheres as the
container lining (`engine.py:2581`), so at a 15 % lining **28 % of pairs** pay 88 µs instead of
4.6 ns. Replace with `gs.sphere_mask` (`(a==b)&(b==c)&(n1==2)&(n2==2)`, invariant under the settle,
which changes size but not shape). Flag-gated: routing a sphere pair through the sphere path changes
rounding and breaks `run2d_shapes`. The `not is_circle` torque guards become
`not (sph[i] and sph[j])`, which is the correct behaviour — a sphere touching a fragment *should*
receive a torque and currently does not.

## Part B — Soft granules that deform (problem 2)

The headline finding: **this needs no new solver.** At 10 kPa a 180 nN bridge equilibrates the
contact at δ = 2.85 µm (r = 40) or 3.59 µm (r = 20); at 3 GPa it equilibrates at **0.001 µm**. The
Hertz/JKR law already produces the deformation. V3.1 removed it twice over — by choosing PMMA, and
by setting `max_overlap_frac` to 0.03 so the geometric rail bound long before the contact law did.
Part B is about restoring the physics, making the rail traceable, and reporting the result honestly.

### B1. The overlap rail should be derived, not guessed

`_resolve_overlaps` allows `max_overlap_frac · min(r_i, r_j)` of standing overlap and projects away
only the excess. Two regimes:

- `mof·r > δ_eq(F_bridge)` → the projection never fires and the contact equilibrates where
  `F_JKR(δ) = F_bridge`. **This is the physically correct regime.**
- `mof·r < δ_eq(F_bridge)` → the rail binds and caps compaction at a number with no physical
  content. This is what V3.1 did.

So the fix is not a projected-Gauss-Seidel contact solve — it is to size the rail from the contact
law and then stay out of its way. `contact.overlap_model: fixed | elastic` (default `fixed` =
today). Under `elastic`, `setup_to_params` derives

```
max_overlap_frac = clip( safety · δ_eq(F_ref) / r_min , mof_min , 0.30 )
δ_eq(F) = ( 3F / (4 E_s √(r/2)) )^(2/3),   E_s = E_kPa / (2(1−ν²))   [nN/µm²]
```

with `F_ref = expected_bridge_force` (already a Params field), and **prints the derived value with
its inputs** so it is traceable. `max_overlap_frac` reverts to its intended role: a numerical safety
rail against explicit-step overshoot, not a physics parameter.

**The instrument that is missing today.** There is currently no way to know whether the rail fired.
Add `n_overlap_clipped` and `overlap_clip_excess_mean` to both metrics twins. A run in which the
projection fires on a significant fraction of contacts is not reporting contact physics, and the
reader should be able to see that. This is cheap — the `deep` mask already exists at
`reference.py:2103` / `integrate.py:46`.

### B2. Stability — a semi-implicit normal step

Every load-bearing contact is outside the explicit window: the bound is `k < γ/dt` = 2 nN/µm at
r = 20 µm, and at 10 kPa it is crossed at δ ≈ 0.01 µm, F ≈ 0.03 nN. Today the bed is held by the
velocity cap and the projection, neither of which is a contact model.

`contact.semi_implicit` (bool, default False) adds a diagonal implicit treatment of the normal
contact in `step()` (`engine.py:3621,3657`):

```
k_i = Σ_contacts dF/dδ = Σ 2 E_s a            # a = the JKR contact radius, ALREADY returned
vel = F / (γ_i + dt · k_i)                    # instead of F / γ_i
```

Properties: unconditionally stable in the diagonal part; **the fixed point is unchanged** (at
equilibrium `F = 0`, so `Δx = 0` for any damping), so it alters the transient, not the physics
endpoint; per-granule adaptive in effect; and it costs one extra accumulation in the force pass
because `a` is already computed and stored as `c_a[k]`. Accumulate it alongside the existing
per-granule gathers in `gather_2d`/`gather_3d` and both reference twins.

This is what lets the velocity cap stop doing stability duty it was never meant to do.

### B3. The velocity cap is not the bottleneck — but it is an unlabelled distortion

`F_cap = v_max · drag_scale · r` = 20 nN (r = 20, v_max 20) to 60 nN (r = 40, v_max 30), against
180 nN bridges, so granules under bridge load run pinned at the cap. But the *displacement* budget
over 144 steps is 1440–2160 µm and the bed only needs to move ~500 µm, so **the cap is not limiting
total compaction** and is not why V3.1 under-compacted. What it does do is flatten the force
response: granules at 60 nN and 180 nN move identically, so force chains are not resolved.

Recommendation: keep it as a rail, let B2 take over the stability role, and add
`frac_velocity_clipped` to the metrics so a rate-distorted run is visible rather than silent.

### B4. Honest volume bookkeeping

`bed_solid_volume` (`engine.py:3324`) and `phi_solid_true` (`metrics.py:410`) sum nominal
`(4/3)πr³` and double-count every overlap lens. Measured overstatement:

| δ | Z = 6 | Z = 8 | Z = 10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.03 r | 0.10 % | 0.13 % | 0.17 % |
| 0.10 r | 1.11 % | 1.48 % | 1.84 % |
| 0.15 r | 2.47 % | 3.29 % | 4.11 % |

Real but modest — it changes the reported number, not the conclusion. Fix with the existing
`overlap_lens_volume` / `overlap_lens_area` (`engine.py:2057,2076`) and their compiled twins
`lens_area_k` / `lens_volume_k` (`metrics.py:32-56`), which are already computed for `r_eff`, so the
marginal cost is a sum. Emit **new** keys `bed_solid_volume_net`, `phi_bed_net`,
`phi_solid_true_net` and `overlap_volume_fraction`, leaving the existing keys untouched — new keys
do not break `test_legacy_identity`, which iterates the fixture's keys, so no flag is needed.
`overlap_volume_fraction` is the useful one: it says how much of a reported compaction is
interpenetration.

Note `phi_solid` (the rendered field) is already volume-correct — `r_eff` restores the lens and the
JKR clip planes cut the flat faces — it is just inflated ~1.5× by the tanh halo. The V3.1 guidance
that positional metrics are the trustworthy ones needs this amendment.

### B5. Cells binding and tightening the contact

One of the two mechanisms already works: a bridge deepens the contact, which grows `a`, which grows
the friction cap `τ₀·A + µ·F_n`. The other — the cells themselves forming an adhesive collar across
the contact — is not modelled, and JKR pull-off is irrelevant at these scales (0.024–0.19 nN against
50–180 nN of traction).

`cells.bridging.contact_adhesion_nN_per_cell` (`cell_contact_adhesion`, default 0.0). For a contact
spanned by `n_b` mature bridging cells:

```
F_hold    = n_b · w_cell · maturity          # resists separation, added to the JKR adhesive branch
F_t_cap  += F_hold                           # the collar resists sliding as well as pulling apart
```

`n_b` needs no new plumbing: `_service_committed_bridges` already returns `(n_ci, n_cj, fac)` and
the callers already carry it (`reference.py:1352,1486,1691,1804`, `bridging.py:171`). Range 5–50
nN/cell, flagged `[assumption; calibrate]` in the references doc. Legacy-off at 0.0.

This is also the first step toward irreversibility: with it, a compacted contact holds as long as
its bridges do, and bridges already lock in above `bridge_lock_force_threshold`.

### B6. Irreversibility — DEFER, with the reason stated

Real compaction does not spring back; here, removing the cells lets every contact relax elastically.
A proper ratchet needs persistent per-contact state, which the architecture does not carry —
`half_pairs` rebuilds the pair list every step. B5 gives a partial, cell-mediated version. The full
design (an open-addressed `(i,j) → (rest_length, age)` map rebuilt on the neighbour cadence, shared
with the deferred Cundall–Strack spring of A6) is recorded as future work, not built.

### B7. MC-DEM — off by default, and as an explicit sweep axis

`κ = 1 + ν/(1−2ν)·Σδ/2r`, capped at 5. It grows linearly with coordination number: Z = 6 at
δ = 0.05 r gives +68 % repulsion, Z = 8 at δ = 0.15 r gives κ = 3.7. It is physically motivated for
soft gels — that is its origin — but it **opposes compaction**, makes the contact up to 5× stiffer
(worsening B2 by the same factor), and `c_mc = ν/(1−2ν)` is violently sensitive near
incompressibility: 4.5 at ν = 0.45, **24.5 at ν = 0.49**. Default off in the soft-gel presets (as
the PMMA presets already do, though for the opposite reason), present as a named sweep condition,
and whenever it is on, ν must be reported alongside E because the result depends on it more.

### B8. LS-DEM — do not use, and record why

`deformable_enabled` looks like the obvious tool and is not. It forces the **entire run** onto the
Python reference path (`kernels_enabled`, `engine.py:1510`), disabling compiled contacts, cells,
neighbours and overlap resolution for every granule, with `find_contacts_lsdem_2d/3d` running two
Python loops of 128 (2D) / 512 (3D) surface nodes per candidate pair. Worse, it cannot represent the
deformation we need: the mode shapes are **global affine strains about a body-fixed axis**
(`lsdem.py:287-342`), so a granule can only shorten along one axis and cannot flatten at a contact —
and for spheres `is_circle=True` means rotation is never integrated, so that axis can never align
with a contact normal. Wall contacts accumulate no deformation generalised force at all, which is
fatal for a gravity-settled bed whose most loaded contacts are on the floor. It also bypasses the
species tables and the V3.1 stiffness cap for the global `p.E_modulus`, its linear node penalty is
~7× *stiffer* than Hertz at 10 kPa (worse for stability, not better), its modes are non-orthogonal,
and it has zero tests and zero benchmarks. Making it usable means a contact-localised mode basis,
wall coupling and a compiled path — a project, not a phase of V3.2. Record this assessment in the
changelog so it is not re-litigated.

|---|---|---|
| `contact_overlap_model` ('fixed') | `contact.overlap_model` | `fixed` \| `elastic`; derives and prints the rail |

| `cell_contact_adhesion` (0.0) | `cells.bridging.contact_adhesion_nN_per_cell` | cell-mediated hold + tangential cap |

## Part C — An asynchronous cell cycle (problem 3)

### C1. Fix: `cell_age` is never incremented (a V3.1 bug)

`gs.cell_age` is allocated (`engine.py:415`), read (`division.py:67`), reset on division
(`division.py:121`) and serialised — but never advanced. The compiled kernel's parameter named
`cell_age` (`cells.py:241`) is bound to `gs.cell_bridge_age` at the call site (`cells.py:803`);
that collision is why it was missed. **Consequence: with `min_age_h = 8.0` — the shipped default and
the `fibroblast_realistic` value — no cell has ever divided in a real run.**

- Add `age_cells(gs, p)` to `gels/division.py` and call it from both insertion points
  (`reference.py:161`, `cells.py:789`) immediately before `divide_cells`, so the two backends cannot
  drift. One shared function, not two copies.
- Rename the kernel parameter `cell_age` → `cell_bridge_age` at `cells.py:241` to remove the
  collision.
- Aging is invisible to the V2.7 fixtures (`cell_age` is a V3.1 key and `test_legacy_identity`
  iterates the *fixture's* keys), so it runs unconditionally — it is a clock.
- Regression test: a run at the **default** `min_age_h` must produce divisions. The existing
  `test_min_age_delays_division` passes vacuously today and must be rewritten to be meaningful.

### C2. Random starting phase

New per-cell array `cell_cycle_time` (float64, default 0.0 → "use `cell_doubling_time`") in the
`CELL_ARRAYS` registry (`engine.py:397`), so snapshot round-trip and `add_cells` come for free.

At seeding, under `cell_division_enabled`, draw `cell_age ~ U(0, cell_cycle_time)` per cell.

**RNG safety.** `_initialize_cells` (`engine.py:2328`) is the *last* consumer of the packing stream —
`generate_packing`/`generate_packing_3d` return immediately after (`engine.py:2524,2732`) — so a
single guarded vectorised draw **after** the per-granule loop leaves every fixture bit-identical. A
draw *inside* the loop would shift the angle draws of every later granule and break
`run2d_shapes`/`run3d_spheres`. The guard pattern to copy is `division.py:57-62`, gated by
`tests/test_cell_division.py:104`.

Uniform is the right default for a freshly seeded population; the growing-population age density
`f(a) = (2 ln2/T)·2^{−a/T}` is noted in the references doc as the alternative, not implemented.

### C3. Per-cell cycle length

`cells.division.model: poisson | cycle` (`cell_division_model`, default `poisson` = today).

- **`poisson`** — the current memoryless rule, `p_div = (1 − e^{−ln2·dt/T_d})·room`. Cycle times are
  exponential, CV = 1: far more scattered than real fibroblasts (CV ≈ 0.1–0.3).
- **`cycle`** — each cell carries `cell_cycle_time ~ LogNormal(median = T_d, CV = cell_division_cv)`,
  i.e. `T_i = T_d·exp(σZ)`, `σ = √(ln(1+CV²))`, default CV 0.15. A cell divides when
  `cell_age ≥ cell_cycle_time` and its granule has room; parent and daughter each draw a fresh `T_i`.
- **Contact inhibition becomes G0 arrest**, not a probability gate: `cell_age += dt` only where
  `room > 0` (per granule from `capacity_vector`, broadcast through `cell_granule_id`). Cells at
  confluence hold their phase and resume staggered when room appears, instead of bursting.
- Draw accounting: `cycle` mode consumes **zero** draws for the decision and one draw of size
  `2·n_born` for the new cycle times. Both backends share the same `Generator`
  (`division.py` is pure numpy), so they stay in step.
- `test_daughters_start_fresh` (`tests/test_cell_division.py:153`) asserts daughters start at
  `cell_age == 0` — that stays true; the desynchronisation now comes from the drawn cycle length.

### C4. The synchrony generalises beyond division (SHOULD)

Attachment, spreading and FA maturation are pure functions of global simulation time and are
**identical for every granule in the bed** (`reference.py:128-153`, mirrored in `aggregates_k`):
`spread_fraction[i] = min(1, t_since/eff_spread_dur)`, `fa_maturity[i] = min(1, t_since_spread·rate)`,
with per-granule variation only through substrate stiffness. The per-cell state machine then assigns
ATTACHED → SPREADING → PROLIFERATING against those globally timed thresholds
(`reference.py:317-342`). So the whole population matures in lockstep, and the user's complaint is a
symptom of a broader pattern.

Minimal fix: `cells.seeding.clock_jitter_h` (`cell_clock_jitter_h`, default 0.0 = legacy) and a
per-granule `gs.cell_clock_offset ~ U(−j, +j)` subtracted from `t` in those expressions. Per-granule
is the correct unit because the quantities are per-granule; drawn in the same guarded block as C2.

### C5. Diagnostics

`cell_age_mean`, `cell_age_cv` and `division_rate_per_h` into the metrics twins
(`metrics.py`, `reference.py`), and a division-rate panel in `viz2/compare_runs.py` alongside the
existing `n_cells_total` / `n_divisions_cum` series. These are what show asynchrony at a glance.

## Expected outcome, and what would falsify it

Two independent estimates of the ceiling agree, which is worth stating together rather than quoting
one: the **DEM geometric ceiling** `(2/(2−mof))³` = 1.263 at `mof` 0.15 takes φ 0.60 → 0.758, and
the **mean-field** `compute_phi_max_deformable(φ_RCP, E, n2)` already in
`analysis/parameter_sweep.py:218` gives **φ_max = 0.79** at E = 10 kPa, n2 = 3.5, φ_RCP = 0.70. So
the combined shape-plus-deformation ceiling is φ ≈ 0.76–0.79.

With bed height loss `1 − φ₀/φ_final` at fixed solid volume and a free top:

| configuration | φ₀ | φ ceiling | height loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| **V3.1 as shipped** (3 GPa, `mof` 0.03) | 0.60 | 0.628 | **4.5 %** |
| soft spheres, default `mof` 0.15 | 0.60 | 0.758 | 20.8 % |
| + rough, loosely settled start | 0.52 | 0.758 | 31.4 % |
| + irregular fragments (shape ceiling 0.70) | 0.52 | ~0.88 nominal | ~41 % |

The first row is the answer to "why did V3.1 barely compact": choosing PMMA and `mof` 0.03 left the
model **4.5 % of headroom**, and at 3 GPa a 180 nN bridge flattens a contact by 0.001 µm against
2.9–3.6 µm at 10 kPa. Undoing that is the single largest and cheapest gain in this plan.

Three honesty notes on that table.

- Bed **height** loss follows the centre-to-centre packing, so those numbers are meaningful. The
  **φ** in the last two rows is nominal and overlap-double-counted. Measured: at δ = 0.15 r the
  double-count overstates φ by **2.5 % (Z = 6) to 4.1 % (Z = 10)**; at δ = 0.03 r it is 0.1–0.2 %.
  Real but modest — it changes the reported number, not the conclusion.
- Shape and overlap ceilings are not independent, so ~41 % is an upper bracket, not a prediction.
- **A modelling limitation this plan leans on and must state.** GELS treats contact as
  interpenetration with no volume conservation in the force law; only the *rendering* restores the
  lens via `r_eff` and the JKR clip planes. At the 10–15 % overlaps this plan depends on, a real gel
  granule would bulge at the contact rim and that bulge would push on its other neighbours. GELS has
  no such term. So the compaction is right in magnitude and mechanism but the granules are, in
  effect, slightly compressible. Say so in the changelog rather than letting a reader infer volume
  conservation from `r_eff`.

## Verification

1. `python -m unittest discover -s tests -v` green after every phase; `tests/test_legacy_identity.py`
   bit-identical throughout (all new defaults reproduce today).
2. **Deformation delivers compaction** — the same bed under the same cell traction compacts
   measurably more at 10 kPa than at 3 GPa, and the difference tracks the predicted δ_eq.
3. **The packing gain is real, not an artefact** — the support-function certificate shows
   `pen_p95 < 0.05 r_eq` at the reported φ, and the sphere control still lands at 0.55–0.60.
4. **Rotation is the mechanism** — φ_bed with settle rotation exceeds φ_bed without it by > 0.03.
   This is the test that fails loudly if a torque sign is wrong, which is the likeliest bug.
5. **The bed survives the dynamics** — 20 steps with cell forces off must not lose more than 0.01 of
   φ_bed. With `contact.shape_dynamics=False` it should fail dramatically; assert that too, as
   documentation of why the flag exists.
6. **φ is not double-counted** — lens-corrected `phi_bed` on a known overlapping configuration
   matches an analytic value.
7. **Division actually happens** — a run at the *default* `min_age_h` produces divisions, and the
   population grows as `N₀·2^(t/T_d)` within 3 SE over 20 seeds.
8. **Asynchrony** — with `model: cycle`, the division-rate time series has no burst at
   `t = min_age_h`, and `cell_age_cv` at t = 0 is within 10 % of the uniform-distribution value.
9. Viz-layer identity: `compare_runs.py` on the two fixtures produces byte-size-identical legacy
   figures.
10. End to end: `run_showcase.py --sweep compaction --t_total 4` smoke, then the full 72 h set.

**What would falsify the plan.** If phase 3 shows a soft bed under cell traction compacting no more
than the rigid control, the diagnosis in Part B is wrong and the remaining phases should not be
built on it. That check is one sweep condition pair (`soft_spheres` vs `rigid_spheres`) at 24 h and
comes before any of the expensive work — deliberately.
