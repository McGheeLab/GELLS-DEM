# GELLS-DEM V3.4 — the support-function MTD contact solver

Carved out of the V3.3 `robotsim/` adoption plan (`CodeLog/ClaudesPlan/3.3.md`, Phase 3)
and given its own version because it changes the default contact physics for
non-spherical granules. V3.3 closed at Phases 0-2 (twin-drift prerequisites, the periodic
minimum-image guard, and three halo-free structural metrics).

## Why

The V3.2 KNOWN BLOCKER (`CHANGELOG.md:97-133`) is now measured, twice, independently:
at 2 % past first touch `find_contact_superellipsoids_3d` (`gels/engine.py:3095`) detects
**25/200 = 12 %** of true contacts, and over-reports penetration by **median 9.8x,
p90 43x** on the ones it does find.

The cause is structural rather than a tolerance problem:

- `delta` is set to `dp_mag`, the distance between the two common-normal *surface points*.
  That is not a penetration depth.
- the reported normal is `(p_j - p_i)/|.|`, not a surface normal.

So the force is not the gradient of any energy, and no amount of iteration fixes it.

## The replacement

The support function of the GELS anisotropic two-exponent superellipsoid is closed form.
Inverting `superellipsoid_normal` (`engine.py:1278`) gives a nested dual norm

    h(n) = || ( ||(a n_x, b n_y)||_q1 , c n_z ) ||_q2 ,   q1 = n1/(n1-1),  q2 = n2/(n2-1)

Derivation, which also yields everything else the solver needs:

1. At the surface point whose normal is parallel to `n`, the parametric point `p` and the
   *unnormalised* normal `N` satisfy `p . N = cos^2(eta) + sin^2(eta) = 1` identically.
   Hence `h(n_hat) = 1/|N|`.
2. Writing `N = t n_hat` and substituting the parametrisation collapses
   `cos^2(omega)+sin^2(omega)=1` into `A^q1 + B^q1 = cos(eta)^(beta q1)` with
   `A = a n_x t`, `B = b n_y t`, `beta = 2 - 2/n2`, and then
   `cos^2(eta)+sin^2(eta)=1` into `P^q2 + C^q2 = 1`. Solving for `t` gives the norm above.

Verified against a 600x1200 brute-force surface maximisation: **4.9e-6** relative
(mesh-limited) for random `n1 != n2`, **3.6e-15** at the sphere anchor.

This is the exact generalisation of robotsim's superball-only `h(n) = R ||R^T n||_q`
(`robotsim/code/superquadric.py:26`) — the MTD formulation is **not** limited to equal
semi-axes, which is what made the port viable.

Three things fall out of the same intermediates for free:

- **`grad h(n) = ` the support point** (envelope theorem), so the contact point costs nothing.
- **The parametric inversion** `eta = atan2(sgn(n_z) (|c n_z|/h)^(q2/2), (P/h)^(q2/2))`,
  `omega = atan2(sgn(n_y) (|b n_y|/P)^(q1/2), sgn(n_x) (|a n_x|/P)^(q1/2))`, which keeps the
  new solver interoperable with every existing `(eta, omega)` consumer.
- **Exact `R_eff`**: the reverse Gauss map is `x(n_hat) = grad h(n_hat)`, so the principal
  radii are the nonzero eigenvalues of the Hessian `grad^2 h`, and
  `R_eff = sqrt(det grad^2 h |_{n_hat perp})`.

`sep(n) = (c2 - c1).n - h1(n) - h2(n)` is **concave**, so ascent on the unit sphere has a
unique maximum and `sep(n) > 0` at *any* `n` is a certificate of separation.

## Mandatory implementation details

- **Max-factored norms, always.** Never evaluate `|N_x|**q` directly — at `a ~ 40` um and
  `q = 60` that is ~1e96 and the outer level overflows. Factor by the max so every base is
  in [0,1]; the gradient weights are then in [0,1] for free.
- **Degenerate exponents:** `q = n/(n-1)` clamped to `[1+1e-9, 60]`, with `n <= 1` mapped
  straight to 60 (numerically the max-norm, the correct octahedral limit). Explicit `P = 0`
  branch for `n` along +/-z. Guard `h < 1e-300`.
- **Do not replicate `_sgnpow`'s `+1e-30` bias** (`engine.py:1246`) — it would break the
  `p . N = 1` identity the whole derivation rests on.
- **Separation certificate first.** Evaluate `sep(n0)`; `sep(n0) > 0` returns immediately.
  Neighbour lists are generous, so this rejects most candidate pairs in one evaluation —
  that is how the extra iterations are paid for. Today's solver runs 15 unconditional
  iterations on every candidate.
- **Three seeds** (centre line + each body's best-aligned face column of R), keeping the
  best `sep`. Blocky flat faces stall a centre-line ascent on a ridge.
- **Allocation-free.** Today's solver allocates ~8 numpy arrays per iteration
  (`engine.py:3125-3159`); the MTD kernel must be pure scalars, and every early exit must
  return the literal-typed tuple or numba will not unify.
- **Truncation bias is safe:** `sep(n) <= sep(n*)`, so a short ascent gives `delta >= delta_true`
  — slightly too stiff, bounded and continuous, never a missed contact. Assert the residual
  `|g_perp| < 1e-4 (r_i + r_j)` rather than widening the tuple with a `converged` flag.
- **De-duplicate `curvature_R_cap`**, currently copied four times with two different guards
  (`contact3d.py:90` guards `R_eff>0`, `contact2d.py:96` does not). One `@njit` leaf,
  unified on the 3D form.
- **Extend the `validate()` rule** at `config.py:985`: `mtd` + `shape_enabled` +
  blockiness > 2.05 + `curvature_R_cap <= 0` becomes an **error**. Under MTD `R_eff` is the
  *true* ~1e15 um curvature at a flat face rather than FD noise, so the combination is more
  dangerous than it is today.
- **Walls.** `penetration = h_i(-w_hat) - (c_i - q).w_hat`, contact point `= c_i + grad h_i(-w_hat)`.
  Replaces five brute-force samplers (20x20 in 3D, 64-point in 2D) and kills the hardcoded
  `R_local = r_bound * 0.5`. Support-function wall geometry rides with `solver == 'mtd'` to
  keep Gate B clean; `contact.wall_torque` (default `False`) is a separate flag on both
  solvers — `common_normal` already computes its sampled extremum and merely discards it.

## Consequences to document, not to fix

- Shaped contacts become ~30x softer at shallow approach and 3-9x more numerous. That is
  the bug fix, not a regression.
- **MC-DEM effectively switches off for shapes**: `eps_V = sum delta/(2R)` (`contact2d.py:155`)
  drops ~10x with a true `delta`, so `kappa -> 1`. This is *correct* — MC-DEM was being
  driven by a 10x inflated strain. Spheres are unaffected. Record kappa before/after.
- `viz/stress.py:255` magnitudes drop ~30x for shapes; make it read `p.contact_solver`
  rather than assume, and record the solver in `metadata.json`.
- `overlap_model: elastic` (`engine.py:1681`) is a sphere derivation that was never
  applicable to the shaped path; under MTD it becomes valid with **no formula change**.
  Add the test that was previously impossible: a single loaded contact's measured
  equilibrium overlap vs the predicted `delta_eq` within 20 %.
