# V3.5 — gradient flow, and the packer→dynamics handoff fix

## Why this version exists

V3.4 closed with a measured, undisputed finding and a deliberate deferral:

> The packer hands the dynamics a bed pre-loaded by ~2.7e3x the gravitational load,
> because the settle stops on a LENGTH tolerance measured with a proxy that can be
> orders of magnitude too small on the very pair that sets the stopping rule.

`handoff_force_balance()` was landed as the *detector*. V3.5 is the *fix*, plus the
invariant the user asked for alongside it.

Both halves are the same subject — **energy descent** — which is why they share a version:

| Phase | Deliverable |
|---|---|
| **1** | `dynamics.gradient_flow` — the energy audit, and an energy-monotone integrator option |
| **2** | `packing.relax` — FIRE post-settle relaxation with a **force**-based stopping rule (V3.3 Phase 4, rescoped) |

---

## Phase 1 — gradient flow

### The statement being instrumented

Overdamped dynamics is gradient flow. If every force is `-grad E`, then
`gamma x_dot = -grad E` gives

    dE/dt = grad E . x_dot = -gamma |x_dot|^2 <= 0

so **the energy must fall every step**, and the dissipation `D = sum_i gamma_i |v_i|^2 dt`
accounts for the whole of the fall. Any ascent is an integrator failure; any
*discrepancy* between `-dE` and `D` is a force that is not the gradient of the energy
being measured.

That second reading is the more valuable one, and it is what makes this worth building
now rather than in V3.4: **it is the end-to-end certificate for the MTD contact solver.**
V3.4 proved conservativity pointwise (`d(delta)/d(c2) = -n*` to nine places, by the
envelope theorem). It never proved it for the *assembled* force law on a real run. The
audit does, and it is expected to FAIL — visibly and by a known amount — for:

* **MC-DEM** (`kappa` is a state-dependent multiplier on the force, not a term in any
  energy, so `kappa != 1` breaks conservativity by construction);
* the **retired common-normal solver** (`*_cn`), whose normal is not a surface normal;
* **cell bridges**, which are actuators and *should* inject work — that is physics.

So the audit distinguishes "this force law is wrong" from "this force law is active".

### Scope, stated honestly up front

The system is **not** a gradient flow when cells are seeded. Motor-clutch bridges do
work on the assembly. That is not a defect and V3.5 does not try to remove it. The
invariant is exact for the **passive** problem — packing, sedimentation, settling,
relaxation — which is exactly where the V3.4 finding lives.

### The energy

Three terms, all computable with **no kernel change**:

1. **Contact energy.** `ContactSoA` already carries `a_contact`, `R_eff`, `E_star`, `W`
   on every record (the same trick `contact_stiffness_per_granule` uses). Integrating
   the JKR force along the equilibrium branch parameterised by contact radius `a`,

       delta(a) = a^2/R* - sqrt(2 pi W a / E*)
       F(a)     = (4/3) E* a^3/R* - sqrt(8 pi W E* a^3)
       U(a)     = integral F dδ = (8/15) E* a^5/R*^2
                                - (4/3) sqrt(2 pi W E*) a^(7/2)/R*
                                + pi W a^2                        [nN um]

   At `W = 0` and `a = sqrt(R* delta)` this reduces to the Hertz energy
   `(2/5) k delta^(5/2)` with `k = (4/3) E* sqrt(R*)` — the check that pins the algebra.

2. **Wall energy.** Walls are not on the contact list (both gathers apply them inline),
   so this is recomputed from positions. V3.4 made that cheap and exact: one support
   evaluation gives `penetration = h(-w_hat) - (c-q).w_hat`, and the same `U(a)` applies.

3. **Gravitational energy.** `sum_i w_i h_i` with `w_i = granule_weights(gs, p)` — the
   identical array the force path uses, so the two cannot disagree about the sign.

### The modes

One `Params` field, `dynamics_gradient_flow: str`, three values:

| value | behaviour |
|---|---|
| `off` (default) | nothing computed, nothing recorded — bit-identical to V3.4 |
| `monitor` | energy + descent residual every step; **no intervention** |
| `enforce` | as `monitor`, plus backtracking: a step that raises the energy is rejected and retried at `dt/2` |

`enforce` costs a second force evaluation per step. It is the stability guarantee, and it
is only *meaningful* for the passive system — `validate()` warns (does not error) when
cells are seeded, because a user may legitimately want the rail anyway.

### The ascent criterion

An explicit Euler gradient-flow step has a truncation term
`+1/2 dt^2 v^T Hess(E) v`, so a small ascent is expected, not pathological. The
scale-free test is therefore **against the dissipation of the same step**:

    ascent_frac = dE / D,      D = sum_i gamma_i |v_i|^2 dt

`dE <= 0` gives `ascent_frac <= 0`. A benign truncation ascent is `O(dt k / gamma)`.
A blow-up is `>> 1`. `ENERGY_ASCENT_TOL = 0.1` flags a step; `enforce` backtracks on it.

### Metric keys (both twins, via a shared `energy_metrics(gs)` — the
`projection_metrics` precedent, which is already called from both)

`energy_total`, `energy_contact`, `energy_wall`, `energy_gravity`,
`energy_delta`, `energy_dissipation`, `energy_ascent_frac`,
`energy_ascent_steps` (cumulative), `energy_max_ascent_frac`,
`energy_backtracks` (cumulative, `enforce` only).

All plain floats, never NaN (they pass through `csv.DictWriter` and `json.dump`).
Zero throughout when the mode is `off`, so Gate B only ever sees added keys with
constant values.

### Tests — `tests/test_gradient_flow.py`

1. **Hertz limit.** `U(a)` at `W = 0` equals `(2/5) k delta^(5/2)` to 1e-12.
2. **The energy is the potential of the force.** Central-difference `dU/d(delta)` against
   `jkr_force_from_overlap` over a sweep of `delta`, `R*`, `E*`, `W` — this is the
   statement the whole phase rests on.
3. **Two granules, no cells, no MC-DEM: energy falls monotonically** for every step of a
   real run, and `-dE` matches `D` to `O(dt)`.
4. **A settling bed descends.** `energy_ascent_steps == 0` over a passive sedimentation run.
5. **MC-DEM breaks it, by a measured amount.** With `mc_dem_enabled=True` the residual
   `dE + D` is nonzero and scales with `kappa - 1`. Pinned as a measurement, not a bug.
6. **`off` is bit-identical.** Gate A + Gate B + local identity.
7. **`enforce` never raises the energy** — an `assertLessEqual` on every step of a run
   started from a deliberately over-loaded bed (the V3.4 pathological packing).

## Phase 2 — FIRE relaxation and the handoff fix

V3.3's Phase 4 as written would **not** have fixed the V3.4 finding, and this is the
correction that justifies re-planning it rather than executing it:

> "Reuse the settle's own force law, not JKR" — and stop on `max|F| < f_tol` in *that*
> law's units.

The handoff error is a **mismatch between two force laws**. Relaxing to force balance
under the settle's `k_rep ov^1.5` says nothing about `max|F|` under the dynamics' JKR.
The stopping tolerance has to be expressed in the units of the law that runs next.

So Phase 2 is:

1. **Stop the settle on the true penetration**, not the directional-radius proxy. V3.4
   made `delta` cheap (`se2d_mtd_core` / `se3d_mtd_core`), and the proxy's error tail is
   measured: p10 = 0.45, min = 0.042 of the truth on `fragmented_granules`. This alone
   removes the mechanism.
2. **FIRE post-settle relaxation**, `packing.relax = 'none' | 'fire'`, translations only,
   frozen `g_fire = sched.g_lo`, velocity projection at every wall clamp, and the
   call-count test that keeps the twins' scheduler in sync.
3. **A force-based stopping rule in the dynamics' units**, checked with
   `handoff_force_balance` — the detector V3.4 landed becomes the acceptance test.

**This changes every shaped packing.** It supersedes `run2d_shapes` and requires
re-blessing `v34_2d_shapes`. That is the point of the version, but it must be recorded
in the changelog with the before/after numbers, because after re-blessing the local gate
can no longer see the change.
