# Literature Review: Cellular Potts Model & Hybrid Mesoscale Approaches for Scaffold Modeling

## Purpose

Comprehensive survey of computational frameworks for modeling cell-driven granular scaffold compaction, with the specific goal of identifying the **simplest model** that captures:
1. Stochastic cell bridging between packed granules
2. Forces those bridges exert
3. Resulting granule rearrangement
4. Runtime in seconds, not hours

---

## 1. Cellular Potts Model (CPM / Glazier-Graner-Hogeweg)

### Core Algorithm

The CPM discretizes space into a regular lattice where each biological cell occupies multiple lattice sites (pixels/voxels) sharing the same cell ID (spin sigma). The system evolves via Metropolis dynamics.

**Hamiltonian (energy function):**

```
H = sum_{<i,j>} J(tau(sigma_i), tau(sigma_j)) (1 - delta(sigma_i, sigma_j))
  + sum_sigma lambda_V (V(sigma) - V_target(sigma))^2
  + sum_sigma lambda_S (S(sigma) - S_target(sigma))^2
  + H_chemotaxis + H_extensions
```

Term-by-term:
- **Adhesion energy**: J(tau_i, tau_j) is boundary energy per unit contact area between cell types tau_i and tau_j. Lower J = stronger adhesion. Summed over all neighboring lattice site pairs where the cells differ.
- **Volume constraint**: lambda_V (V - V_target)^2. Penalizes deviation from target volume. Acts as effective pressure: P = 2*lambda_V*(V - V_target).
- **Surface area constraint**: lambda_S (S - S_target)^2. Penalizes deviation from target perimeter/surface area. Controls membrane tension.
- **Chemotaxis**: dH_chem = -lambda_chem [c(x_dest)/(s + c(x_dest)) - c(x_source)/(s + c(x_source))] where c is chemical concentration and s is saturation.

**Metropolis dynamics (per Monte Carlo Step, MCS):**
1. Randomly select a lattice site (source) and one of its neighbors (target)
2. Propose copying the target's spin to the source
3. Compute energy change dH from this copy
4. Accept with probability: P = min(1, exp(-dH / T_m)) where T_m controls membrane fluctuation amplitude
5. One MCS = N attempted copies, where N = total lattice sites

**Time mapping**: 1 MCS ~ 1 minute (calibrated from cell speed: ~0.1 pixel/MCS, experimental ~0.4 um/min with 4 um pixels).

**Cell migration** emerges from boundary fluctuations -- not explicitly programmed. The dH/T_m ratio governs motility: low ratio = highly motile, high ratio = rigid/immotile.

**Cell proliferation**: Target volume grows over time via Python steppables; when V >= 2*V_initial, cell divides along a random axis into two daughters.

### Key References
- Graner & Glazier (1992) "Simulation of biological cell sorting using a two-dimensional extended Potts model." *Physical Review Letters* 69:2013-2016.
- Glazier & Graner (1993) "Simulation of the differential adhesion driven rearrangement of biological cells." *Physical Review E* 47:2128-2154.
- Swat et al. (2012) "Multi-Scale Modeling of Tissues Using CompuCell3D." *Methods in Cell Biology* 110:325-366.
- Wortel & Textor (2021) "Artistoo, a library to build, share, and explore simulations of cells and tissues in the web browser." *eLife* 10:e61288.

### Relevance to GELS

**Pros:**
- Natural framework for differential adhesion and cell sorting
- Explicit cell shape representation (each cell is many pixels)
- Mature software ecosystem (CompuCell3D, Morpheus, CellularPotts.jl)
- Good for morphogenesis, tissue self-organization

**Cons -- critical for our application:**
- **Lattice resolution problem**: Each granule (R=40 um) would need ~25-75 pixels per cell on a 4 um lattice. For 500 granules in 2D, that is a 1000x1000+ lattice with ~500k pixels. Each MCS does 500k random copy attempts. Even at 1000 MCS per simulated minute, simulating 72 hours = 4.3M MCS = 2.15 trillion pixel operations. This is computationally prohibitive.
- **No native mechanics**: CPM does not solve force balance equations. Forces are emergent from energy minimization, not prescribed. Hertz contact, JKR adhesion, motor-clutch traction -- none of these have natural CPM representations.
- **Granules are not cells**: CPM represents each cell as a deformable patch of pixels. But our granules are rigid (or nearly rigid) particles with prescribed contact mechanics. Representing a rigid hydrogel bead as a CPM "cell" with volume/surface constraints is an impedance mismatch -- we would be fighting the framework.
- **Cell bridges are not adhesion energies**: Our bridges are discrete, stochastic, time-dependent force-transmitting elements with a state machine (PROLIFERATING -> MIGRATING -> BRIDGING -> SENESCENT). CPM adhesion is a static energy parameter J(tau_i, tau_j) -- no dynamics, no force magnitude, no state machine.
- **Speed**: Serial CPM on a 1000^2 lattice: ~0.01 s/MCS (optimistic). 72h biological time at 1000 MCS/min = 4.3M MCS = ~12 hours wall time. GPU-accelerated: ~30x faster = ~25 min. Still too slow for parameter sweeps.

**Verdict: CPM is a poor fit for this problem.** The physics of packed rigid granules with active cell bridges maps much more naturally onto a particle-based (DEM/center-based) model than a lattice-based energy minimization.

## 2. CompuCell3D (CC3D)

### Overview
CompuCell3D is the standard CPM software, led by James Glazier (Indiana University). It provides a GUI + Python scripting interface for building multi-scale CPM simulations.

**Architecture:**
- Lattice-based GGH/CPM core
- PDE solver for diffusing fields (FlexibleDiffusionSolverFE)
- Python steppables for custom logic at each MCS
- Plugin system for energy terms (Volume, Surface, Adhesion, Chemotaxis, etc.)
- Supports 2D and 3D

**Typical cell representation:**
- 25-74 voxels per cell (2D)
- Voxel size: 2-4 um
- Maximum practical scale: ~10^3-10^4 cells on desktop

### Key References
- Swat et al. (2012) "Multi-Scale Modeling of Tissues Using CompuCell3D." *Methods in Cell Biology* 110:325-366.
- Izaguirre et al. (2004) "CompuCell, a multi-model framework for simulation of morphogenesis." *Bioinformatics* 20:1129-1137.

### Relevance to GELS

CC3D would require us to:
1. Represent each granule as a CPM "cell" with high stiffness (large lambda_V, lambda_S)
2. Represent biological cells as separate CPM entities on granule surfaces
3. Implement bridge mechanics as custom energy terms or steppables
4. Fight the framework at every step to maintain rigid granule shapes

**Verdict: Not recommended.** CC3D excels at soft, deformable cells doing morphogenesis. It is the wrong tool for rigid particle mechanics with prescribed contact laws.

## 3. Hybrid CPM + Mechanical Models

### CPM + Bead-Spring ECM (van Oers et al. 2023)

The most relevant hybrid approach couples a CPM (for cell decisions) with a molecular dynamics bead-spring network (for ECM fiber mechanics).

**Architecture:**
- CPM lattice (900x900 sites, 0.25 um/pixel) for cell shape and motility
- Bead-spring fiber network (HOOMD-blue molecular dynamics) for ECM
- Coupling via "adhesion particles" -- ECM beads mechanically clamped to CPM boundary sites
- Alternating timesteps: 1 CPM MCS, then 200 MD steps

**Cell-ECM coupling:**
- Cell contraction via target area A_0 = 1 pixel (drives isotropic contraction)
- Adhesion particles transmit force: dH_ECM = (F_hat_i - F_i) . (p_hat - p)
- Traction forces: 10^-6 to 10^-8 N, matching experiments

**ECM mechanical model:**
- Intra-fiber springs: U_s = 0.5 * k_s * (dr)^2
- Cross-linker springs: U_c = 0.5 * k_c * (dr)^2
- Angular bending: U_b = 0.5 * k_b * (dtheta)^2
- Overdamped Langevin: gamma * dx_i = F_i + F_cell_i + W_i
- Bulk moduli: 0.86-22 kPa (matching collagen/fibrin gels)

**Key finding:** Force propagation follows power law r^(-n) with n = 0.35-0.65, matching viscoelastic ECM experiments. Forces reach 8 cell diameters (~200 um).

**Runtime:** "Computationally intensive" -- ~8 hours for 10,000 timesteps on a single cell in a fiber network.

### Key References
- van Oers et al. (2023) "Hybrid cellular Potts and bead-spring modeling of cells in fibrous extracellular matrix." *Biophysical Journal* 122:2609-2625.
- Hirway et al. (2021) "Multicellular mechanochemical hybrid cellular Potts model of tissue formation during epithelial-mesenchymal transition." *Computational and Systems Oncology* 1:e1031.
- Rens & Merks (2020) "A hybrid model of intercellular tension and cell-matrix mechanical interactions in a multicellular geometry." *Biomechanics and Modeling in Mechanobiology* 19:1747-1764.

### Relevance to GELS

**Pros:**
- Demonstrates that CPM can be coupled to mechanical solvers
- ECM bead-spring is conceptually similar to granule contact network

**Cons:**
- Still uses CPM for cell decisions (same lattice overhead)
- 8 hours for a single cell -- orders of magnitude too slow
- The ECM is a continuous fiber network, not discrete granules
- Coupling protocol (alternating CPM/MD) adds complexity without benefit for our problem

**Verdict: Interesting science, wrong application.** The hybrid concept (cell decisions + mechanical solver) is sound, but the CPM component is unnecessary baggage for our granule-bridge problem.

## 4. Morpheus

### Overview
Morpheus is a GUI-based modeling environment (TU Dresden) that supports CPM, ODEs, PDEs, and their coupling. XML-based model specification -- no programming required for basic models.

**Key features:**
- Declarative modeling via GUI (no code)
- CPM + reaction-diffusion systems
- ODE/SDE integration per cell
- SBML model import
- 2D and 3D support
- Open source (BSD license, GitLab)

### Key References
- Starruss et al. (2014) "Morpheus: a user-friendly modeling environment for multiscale and multicellular systems biology." *Bioinformatics* 30:1331-1332.

### Relevance to GELS

Same fundamental limitations as CompuCell3D -- it is a CPM framework. The declarative (no-code) approach is actually a disadvantage for our custom mechanics: we need fine-grained control over contact forces, bridge state machines, and packing algorithms that cannot be expressed in Morpheus's XML schema.

**Verdict: Not suitable.** GUI-based CPM frameworks optimize for accessibility, not for custom particle mechanics.

## 5. Chaste (Cancer, Heart and Soft Tissue Environment)

### Overview
Chaste (University of Oxford) is a C++ library supporting multiple cell-based model types: cellular automata, CPM, center-based (overlapping spheres and Voronoi), and vertex models. It is the most flexible framework in terms of model type variety.

**Supported model types:**
| Type | Representation | Force model |
|------|---------------|-------------|
| Cellular Automaton | 1 lattice site per cell | Rule-based |
| Cellular Potts | Multiple sites per cell | Energy-based (Hamiltonian) |
| Overlapping Spheres | Center + radius | Pairwise spring forces |
| Voronoi | Center + Voronoi tessellation | Pairwise spring forces |
| Vertex | Polygon vertices | Energy-based (area + perimeter) |

**Overlapping spheres force law (most relevant to GELS):**
```
F_ij(r) = mu * log(1 + (r - s))           if r <= s  (compression)
         = mu * (r - s) * exp(-alpha*(r-s))  if s < r <= r_A  (adhesion)
         = 0                                  if r > r_A
```
where s = rest length, r_A = adhesion range, mu = spring constant, alpha = adhesion decay.

**Computational performance (from Comparing Individual-Based Approaches, Osborne et al. 2017):**
| Case | Cellular Automaton | CPM | Overlapping Spheres | Vertex |
|------|-------------------|-----|--------------------| ------|
| Adhesion | 34 s | 1638 s | 168 s | 321 s |
| Proliferation | 44 s | 4336 s | 1218 s | 2419 s |
| Short-range signaling | 166 s | 7536 s | 2837 s | 9983 s |
| Long-range signaling | 1393 s | 23741 s | 804 s | 5803 s |

Key takeaway: **CPM is 10-50x slower than overlapping spheres** across all benchmarks.

### Key References
- Mirams et al. (2013) "Chaste: An Open Source C++ Library for Computational Physiology and Biology." *PLoS Computational Biology* 9:e1002970.
- Osborne et al. (2017) "Comparing individual-based approaches to modelling the self-organization of multicellular tissues." *PLoS Computational Biology* 13:e1005387.
- Pitt-Francis et al. (2009) "Chaste: a test-driven approach to software development for biological modelling." *Computer Physics Communications* 180:2452-2471.

### Relevance to GELS

**Pros:**
- Supports overlapping spheres (center-based) model -- conceptually identical to our DEM
- Well-validated against multiple model types
- C++ performance

**Cons:**
- C++ library with steep learning curve
- Heavy framework overhead for what we need
- No JKR contact, no motor-clutch, no quaternion rotational dynamics
- Would require significant custom code anyway
- Not Python-native

**Verdict: The Chaste benchmarks confirm that center-based (overlapping sphere) models are the right architecture for our problem, but Chaste itself is too heavy a framework. Our existing `new_dem_0.py` is already a specialized, high-performance overlapping-sphere DEM.**

## 6. PhysiCell

### Overview
PhysiCell (Macklin lab) is an open-source, agent-based, off-lattice simulator for 3D multicellular systems. It is the closest existing framework to what GELS does.

**Core algorithm:**
- Each cell = center point + radius (overlapping spheres)
- Inertialess (overdamped) force balance: v_i = sum(F_adhesion + F_repulsion + F_motility) / gamma
- Chemical microenvironment via BioFVM (reaction-diffusion PDE solver)
- Three timescales: dt_diff (0.01 min), dt_mech (0.1 min), dt_cells (6 min)

**Force model (Piecewise Quadratic, PWQ):**
```
F_PWQ(r) = mu_A*(1 - r/r_A)^2 - mu_R*(1 - r/r_R)^2   if r <= r_R  (repulsion + adhesion)
         = mu_A*(1 - r/r_A)^2                           if r_R < r <= r_A  (adhesion only)
         = 0                                              otherwise
```

**Position update:** Second-order Adams-Bashforth.

**Performance:**
- Linear scaling in cell count (via spatial interaction data structure)
- Desktop (quad-core): 10^5 to 10^6 cells
- HPC: ~2 million cells
- Simulating 10-30 days in ~3 wall-clock days for large systems

**ECM framework (Metzcar et al. 2024):** Each ECM element stores (orientation, anisotropy, density). Cells remodel local ECM; ECM modulates cell motility. Demonstrated for invasion, wound healing, collective migration. Runtime: 3-13 minutes on desktop for ~500-900 cells over 5-15 days biological time.

### Key References
- Ghaffarizadeh et al. (2018) "PhysiCell: An open source physics-based cell simulator for 3-D multicellular systems." *PLoS Computational Biology* 14:e1005991.
- Metzcar et al. (2024) "A Simple Framework for Agent-Based Modeling with Extracellular Matrix." *Bulletin of Mathematical Biology* 87:31.
- Heiland et al. (2024) "PhysiCell Studio: a graphical tool to make agent-based modeling more accessible." *GigaScience* 12:giad084.

### Relevance to GELS

**Pros:**
- Same overdamped, center-based, off-lattice architecture as GELS
- Proven scalability to 10^6 cells
- ECM interaction framework exists
- Active development community

**Cons:**
- C++ codebase (not Python)
- Force model is generic (piecewise quadratic), not Hertz/JKR
- No superellipsoid shapes, no quaternion rotational dynamics
- No motor-clutch model
- No bridge state machine
- Would require extensive customization to match GELS physics
- Already outperformed by our purpose-built code for this specific problem

**Verdict: PhysiCell validates our architectural choices (overdamped, center-based, off-lattice) but offers no advantage over our existing code.** The ECM interaction framework is interesting conceptually but models a continuum ECM, not discrete granular contacts. GELS already has all the physics PhysiCell lacks (JKR, motor-clutch, superellipsoids, bridge state machine).

## 7. CBMOS (Center-Based Model Overlapping Spheres)

### Overview
CBMOS is a Python framework (MIT license) specifically designed for numerical study of center-based models. GPU-accelerated via CuPy.

**Force functions implemented:**
- Cubic (MecaGen): F = mu*(r - r_A)^2*(r - s)
- Piecewise Quadratic (PhysiCell): F = mu_A*(1-r/r_A)^2 - mu_R*(1-r/r_R)^2
- Generalized Linear Spring (Chaste): F = mu*log(1+(r-s)) [compression] or mu*(r-s)*exp(-alpha*(r-s)) [adhesion]
- Hertz contact (native)

**ODE solvers:** Forward Euler, Midpoint, Heun, Adams-Bashforth (2nd order), Backward Euler (implicit).

**Performance:**
- CPU (NumPy): reasonable for 100 cells, several minutes for 10,000
- GPU (CuPy): ~30x speedup, 10,000 cells in ~4 seconds
- O(n^2) all-pairs evaluation (faster than bounding-box for <10,000 cells in Python)

### Key References
- Mathias et al. (2022) "CBMOS: a GPU-enabled Python framework for the numerical study of center-based models." *BMC Bioinformatics* 23:55.

### Relevance to GELS

**Pros:**
- Pure Python, GPU-accelerated
- Already implements Hertz contact
- Clean numerical study framework
- Same architectural paradigm as GELS

**Cons:**
- No JKR, no motor-clutch, no bridge state machine
- No superellipsoid support
- Limited to ~10,000 cells before GPU memory limits
- Would need extensive extension for our physics

**Verdict: Confirms that Python center-based models with Hertz contact can be fast enough. GELS already implements everything CBMOS has and more.**

## 8. SimuCell3D

### Overview
SimuCell3D (2024, *Nature Computational Science*) simulates tissue mechanics with subcellular resolution using deformable cell surfaces in 3D. Each cell represented by a triangulated surface mesh.

**Key innovation:** Overcomes the classical trade-off between spatial resolution and cell count. Can simulate tens of thousands of cells with high spatial resolution.

**Physics:** Surface tension, intercellular adhesion, polarized cell mechanics, nuclear mechanics.

### Key References
- Taubenberger et al. (2024) "SimuCell3D: three-dimensional simulation of tissue mechanics with cell polarization." *Nature Computational Science* 4:318-331.

### Relevance to GELS
Designed for epithelial tissues (tightly-packed deformable cells), not granular packings with void space and bridges. Wrong application domain.

**Verdict: Not relevant.**

## 9. DEM Framework for ECM, Deformable Cells, and Subcellular Components (Sandersius et al. 2015)

### Overview
A DEM approach where cells are represented by multiple particles (cytoplasm, membrane, nucleus) and ECM is a particle network. Uses Hertzian contact + linear adhesion + viscous damping.

**Key features:**
- Multi-particle cell representation (deformable)
- Hertz contact for compression
- Linear springs for adhesion (cadherin, integrin)
- Explicit Verlet integration
- GPU-parallelizable

### Key References
- Sandersius et al. (2015) "Discrete Element Framework for Modelling Extracellular Matrix, Deformable Cells and Subcellular Components." *PLoS Computational Biology* 11:e1004544.

### Relevance to GELS

**Pros:**
- DEM with Hertz contact (same as us)
- Natural handling of cell-ECM interactions
- GPU-parallelizable

**Cons:**
- Multi-particle cells add enormous complexity
- Overkill for our problem -- we do not need subcellular resolution
- Significantly slower than single-particle-per-granule approach

**Verdict: Interesting but over-resolved for our needs.**

## 10. Agent-Based Model of Collagen Gel Compaction

### Overview
Stevenson et al. (2010) and related work model fibroblast-mediated collagen gel compaction using agent-based discrete fiber network models. Fibrils = strings of nodes connected by Hookean springs. Cells apply traction forces to nearby fibrils.

**Key finding:** Fiber network undergoes macroscopic compaction with alignment between cells. Fibril density increases preferentially in pericellular regions, matching experimental "matrical tracks."

### Key References
- Stevenson et al. (2010) "Pericellular conditions regulate extent of cell-mediated compaction of collagen gels." *Biophysical Journal* 99:19-28.
- Abhilash et al. (2014) "Remodeling of fibrous extracellular matrices by contractile cells: predictions from discrete fiber network simulations." *Biophysical Journal* 107:1829-1840.
- Lee et al. (2018) "An agent-based discrete collagen fiber network model of dynamic traction force-induced remodeling." *Journal of Biomechanical Engineering* 140:051003.

### Relevance to GELS

Conceptually closest to our problem: cells applying traction forces that compact a discrete material network. However, their "network" is continuous collagen fibers, not discrete granules. The compaction mechanism differs: fiber densification and alignment vs. granule rearrangement via bridge forces.

**Verdict: Validates our modeling approach (discrete agents + mechanical network = compaction), but the continuous fiber formulation does not apply to granular systems.**

## Recommendation: The V2.5 Contact Network IS the Right Approach

After surveying the entire landscape, the conclusion is clear: **none of the existing frameworks are suitable for this problem, and the V2.5 Contact Network model already planned is the optimal approach.**

### Why CPM Fails Here

The fundamental mismatch: CPM represents soft, deformable entities evolving via energy minimization. Our system has:
- **Rigid granules** with prescribed contact mechanics (JKR)
- **Active cell bridges** with stochastic formation, force ramp, state machine
- **Overdamped force balance** (not energy minimization)
- **Specific force laws** (motor-clutch, not generic adhesion)

Forcing this into a CPM framework would be like using a word processor to write code -- technically possible but fighting the tool at every step.

### Why the Contact Network Model is Optimal

The V2.5 Contact Network model (already planned in `V2.5_mesoscale_models.md`) is essentially a **simplified, graph-based DEM** that:
1. Represents each granule as a node (position, radius, type, cell count)
2. Represents contacts and bridges as edges (overlap, gap, bridge state, force)
3. Uses the same physics as GELS (Hertz contact, motor-clutch bridges, overdamped dynamics)
4. Drops expensive operations (superellipsoid contact solver, field rendering, per-cell tracking)
5. Keeps spheres only (analytic contact, no Newton-Raphson)

**Expected performance:**
- 500 spheres with cKDTree neighbor search: ~0.1 ms/step
- 72 hours at dt=0.5h = 144 steps: ~15 ms total
- Including bridge state updates and force balance: ~100-500 ms total
- Monte Carlo ensemble of 1000 realizations: ~100-500 seconds
- **This meets the "seconds, not hours" target.**

### What We Gain from This Survey

1. **Validation of architecture**: PhysiCell (>10^6 cells), CBMOS (GPU-accelerated), and Chaste benchmarks all confirm that overdamped center-based models with pairwise forces are the right paradigm for speed + accuracy.

2. **Confidence in dropping CPM**: The Osborne et al. 2017 comparison shows CPM is 10-50x slower than overlapping spheres for equivalent biology. Since we need custom mechanics anyway, the CPM overhead buys us nothing.

3. **ECM interaction pattern from PhysiCell/Metzcar**: The idea of representing ECM elements with (orientation, anisotropy, density) could inform future extensions of the Contact Network model to track local tissue architecture at each node.

4. **Bridge mechanics from hybrid CPM+MD literature**: The van Oers et al. 2023 finding that traction force propagation follows power-law decay r^(-0.35 to -0.65) provides a validation target for our bridge force transmission model.

5. **Motor-clutch as the right force model**: The master equation analysis (Bangasser et al. 2013) confirms that ODE solutions of the motor-clutch model agree with full stochastic Monte Carlo and are orders of magnitude faster -- exactly what we already use.

### Concrete Next Step

Proceed with implementing `analysis/contact_network.py` as specified in `V2.5_mesoscale_models.md`. No framework adoption needed. The simplest model that captures stochastic bridging, bridge forces, and granule rearrangement is a **sphere-only, graph-based DEM with the bridge state machine from GELS V2.3**. This is exactly what the V2.5 Contact Network model is.
