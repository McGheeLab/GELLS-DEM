# V2.2 MC-DEM Implementation & V3.0 LS-DEM Upgrade Roadmap

**Date:** 2026-03-16
**Status:** V2.2 implemented; V3.0 planned

---

## V2.2: Multi-Contact DEM (MC-DEM) — IMPLEMENTED

### What Changed

Added stress-based multi-contact correction to Hertzian contact forces
(Giannis et al. 2021, *Granular Matter*). When a soft hydrogel granule
has multiple simultaneous contacts (which it always does in a jammed
packing), each contact sees a stiffer response due to volumetric
confinement. Standard Hertz treats each contact independently and
underestimates forces.

### Physics

Per-particle volumetric strain from all contacts:
```
ε_V,i = Σ_c δ_c / (2 R_i)
```

Confinement correction factor:
```
κ_i = 1 + ν/(1−2ν) × ε_V,i
```

For each contact (i,j), the Hertz repulsion is scaled by κ_ij = (κ_i + κ_j)/2.
Only the repulsive component is scaled; adhesion and friction are unchanged.

For hydrogels at ν = 0.49: ν/(1−2ν) = 24.5. A particle with 7 contacts at
~1% overlap strain sees κ ≈ 1.9 (nearly 2× stiffening). Capped at κ_max = 5.0.

### Implementation

- `mc_dem_correction()` function in `new_dem_0.py`
- Called after the standard contact loop in both `compute_forces()` (2D) and
  `compute_forces_3d()` (3D)
- Post-correction approach: standard forces computed first, then correction
  applied as additional force based on per-particle confinement state
- New Params: `mc_dem_enabled` (bool, default True), `mc_dem_kappa_max` (float, default 5.0)
- Per-contact `kappa` stored for visualization

### Computational Cost

~1.5× overhead per timestep: one extra pass through the contacts list to
compute ε_V, then another pass to apply corrections. No iterative solver needed.

### Validation

Smoke-tested with DOE_0001 (φ_solid=0.692, ν=0.49, 356 granules, periodic BCs).
Simulation runs stably. Slightly higher displacements than without MC-DEM,
consistent with stronger contact forces driving more compaction.

## V3.0: Level-Set DEM (LS-DEM) with Deformable Particles — PLANNED

### Motivation

MC-DEM corrects the force magnitude but particles remain rigid shapes.
For soft hydrogels at high packing fractions (φ > 0.65), granules
physically deform: they flatten at contacts, change shape, and can even
form new contacts as they spread. This isn't captured by MC-DEM.

### Target Approach: Variational LS-DEM (Henzel & Karapiperis 2026)

> T. Henzel, K. Karapiperis, "A Variational Formulation for Deformable
> Particle Simulations and its Level Set Discrete Element Method
> Implementation," arXiv:2602.12895, 2026.

Key features:
- Energetic variational formulation (Lagrange-d'Alembert principle)
- Translation + rotation + deformation DOFs per particle
- Deformation via evolving level sets (not mesh-based)
- **Claims same computational order as rigid DEM** (unlike MPFEM at 100-1000×)
- Not restricted to specific geometries

### Alternative Approaches to Consider

| Method | Cost | Fidelity | Reference |
|--------|------|----------|-----------|
| **Variational LS-DEM** | ~1-2× rigid | Full shape change | Henzel 2026 |
| DDEM (global+local modes) | ~2-5× | Moderate deformation | Rojek 2021 |
| SDF-DEM contact detection | ~1.5× | Better contact accuracy | Lai 2022/2024 |
| MPFEM | ~100-1000× | Full FEM per particle | Not feasible for us |

### Recommended V3.0 Upgrade Path

1. **Phase 1: SDF-based contact detection** (replaces Newton-Raphson)
   - Implement analytical SDF for superellipsoids
   - GDF minimisation for contact point (Gouveia 2025 approach, extend to 3D)
   - Expected benefit: more robust for blocky shapes, slight speedup

2. **Phase 2: Level-set particle representation**
   - Replace (a, b, c, n1, n2, quat) with level-set φ(x) per particle
   - Initialise level sets from superellipsoid SDF
   - Retain rigid-body dynamics initially (validate against V2.2)

3. **Phase 3: Deformation DOFs**
   - Add shape deformation modes per particle (variational formulation)
   - Level sets evolve under contact forces
   - Validate: particles should flatten at contacts, Z should increase
   - Compare compaction curves against MC-DEM (V2.2) and experimental data

4. **Phase 4: New contact formation**
   - As particles deform, new contacts form (flat face → larger contact area)
   - This is the key physical effect missing from both rigid DEM and MC-DEM
   - Validate against Brodu 2015 hydrogel force imaging data

### Key Implementation Questions

- **Memory**: each level set needs a 3D grid per particle. At N=356 granules
  and ~20³ grid per particle, that's 356 × 8000 = 2.8M floats (~22 MB).
  Feasible but may need optimisation for larger systems.
- **Numba/JIT**: level-set operations are naturally parallelisable and
  amenable to Numba vectorisation
- **Backwards compatibility**: V3.0 should support both rigid (V2.2) and
  deformable particles, with a `deformable_enabled` flag

### Experimental Validation Targets

- Hirsch et al. 2022 (*Science Advances*): microgel compression data
- Brodu et al. 2015 (*Nature Communications*): 3D force imaging in
  deformable hydrogel packings
- Feng et al. 2025 (*Adv. Healthcare Materials*): granular hydrogel
  scaffold design guide
