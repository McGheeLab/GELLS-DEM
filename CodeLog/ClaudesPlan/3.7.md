# V3.7 — the physics the figures were doing by themselves

## Why this version exists

A sweep of `viz/`, `viz2/` and `analysis/` for machinery stronger than the
engine's turned up the subject of this version, and it runs in **two
directions at once**.

**One thing the visualization code has that the engine does not.**
`analysis/coarse_grain.py` computes a Love-Weber (virial) stress tensor from
the contact forces. The engine has no stress at all: `grep -rn
"virial\|stress_tensor\|pressure" gels/` returns **zero hits**, and none of the
130 metric keys is a stress. Everything mechanical the engine reports is a
force — `F_mean`, `F_max`, `F_func_mean` — which is extensive, scale-dependent
and not comparable to any measurement. A pressure is intensive, is in kPa, and
splits into the part the granules carry and the part the cells are generating,
which is the question this model exists to answer. It is also the signal
robotsim's arrest detector used (`P_vir`) and which V3.6 had to substitute
`F_mean` for.

**Three things the visualization code is doing for itself that the engine
already knows — and getting wrong.** `viz2/energy_stress.py`, `viz/stress.py`
and `analysis/coarse_grain.py` each independently reconstruct contact physics
from the **global** `p.E_modulus` with Hertz (and, in `coarse_grain`, a DMT
adhesion term retired in V2.3). Measured, not assumed:

| | |
|---|---|
| `viz2` contact energy vs the engine's JKR energy | **790x** too large |
| — of which: the missing kPa -> nN/um^2 conversion | x1000 |
| — of which: Hertz prefactor `(2/5)` where the true one is `(8/15)` | x0.75 |
| — remainder: Hertz where the contact is JKR, at shallow overlap | x0.73 |
| The engine's `jkr_contact_energy` against a numerical `int F dd` | **1.00000** |
| PMMA preset: E* assumed by viz vs the per-pair E* the engine stored | 1.74e9 vs 5.79e4 kPa (**3e4x**) |

The root cause is one line of bookkeeping: the snapshot writes ten contact
columns and stops. `a_contact`, `E_star`, `W` and `kappa` are computed every
step, held in `ContactSoA`, and **never serialized** — so no downstream module
*can* use the engine's own contact energy, and each one guessed instead.

There is a second-order consequence that matters more than the factor of 790.
The six panels of the energy-mode figure are in **four different unit
systems**: nN um (traction, frustration), nN um/h (friction, which is a
*power*), um^-2 (osmotic, which carries no energy scale at all) and
750 x nN um (contact). The figure exists to show which energy mode dominates,
and what it plots cannot answer that.

## Locked decisions

| Decision | Choice |
|---|---|
| Stress module | `gels/stress.py`, a **leaf** — imports nothing from `gels.engine`, like `convergence.py` / `laguerre.py` / `pore.py`, so engine, pipeline and tests share one implementation |
| Sign convention | **Compression positive** (the granular convention, and robotsim's `P_vir`). Stated in the docstring and pinned by a test |
| Trace | `P = tr(sigma)/D` with **D = 2 in 2D**. `coarse_grain` divides by 3 unconditionally, so its 2D pressure is 2/3 of what it should be |
| Cost | Always on. It is one vectorised pass over columns the contact list already carries — no flag, unlike `metrics_laguerre` |
| `coarse_grain` | **Delegates.** Keeps its signature and its plots; the tensor comes from `gels/stress.py` |
| Osmotic / interfacial | **Relabelled, not re-derived.** `gamma = E_modulus * interface_width * 0.01` has no source; inventing one is a modelling decision, not an engineering fix. They become named indicators and leave the energy axis |

## Phase 1 [DONE] — `gels/stress.py`

Love-Weber over the contact list both twins already build:

    sigma_ab = (1/V) sum_c f_a^c l_b^c,   f = F_normal * n (force on j),
                                          l = x_j - x_i

`n` points from `i` to `j` (`F[i] -= F_n n; F[j] += F_n n`), so a repulsive
contact gives `f . l > 0` and `tr(sigma) > 0`: **compression positive**.

The active part is the same sum over bridging cells, with `f` the per-cell
force and `l` from the host granule to the bridge target. This is the
decomposition the version is for — `P_active / P_vir` is the fraction of the
bed's stress the cells are holding up.

Keys: `P_vir`, `P_contact`, `P_active`, `stress_von_mises`, `stress_dev`,
`stress_anisotropy`, `stress_active_frac`. Every value a plain float, no NaN
(they pass through `csv.DictWriter`), and present in **both** twins.

`V` is `domain_volume(p)` corrected for boundary exclusion, exactly as
`coarse_grain._inner_volume` does — but taken from the engine's shape-aware
`domain_volume`, so a cylinder and a free top are right without a second
implementation.

## Phase 2 [DONE] — the contact columns reach the snapshot

Add `a_contact`, `E_star`, `W`, `kappa` to the serialized set. Measured cost:
the contact arrays are ~5 % of a snapshot, so four more float64 columns on top
of ten is about **+2 %** of the file.

`kappa` is included because CLAUDE.md already records MC-DEM's confinement
factor as a thing to watch, and it is the one contact quantity with no
recomputable proxy.

The reference path writes list-of-dicts and the kernel path writes
`ContactSoA`; both must emit the same names, and the LS-DEM branch does not
carry `E_star`/`W` at all, so the writer must tolerate their absence rather
than assume the key.

## Phase 3 [DONE] — the suite uses the engine's physics

* `viz2/energy_stress.py`: the contact panel calls the engine's
  `jkr_contact_energy` on the stored columns; the traction panel uses V3.6's
  `U = F^2 / 2k` with `1/k = 1/k_cell + 1/k_sub`; friction is relabelled a
  dissipation **rate**; osmotic and interfacial are relabelled indicators.
  Frustration stops being a separate mode — it was `|F| * overlap` on
  inert-involving contacts, i.e. a *subset* of the contact energy, so it
  double-counted with the contact panel.
* `viz/stress.py`: stop recomputing Hertz from the global modulus when
  `contact_F_normal` is in the snapshot. Its reconstruction path also still
  calls the **V3.4-retired** common-normal solver names.
* `analysis/coarse_grain.py`: delegate the tensor; drop the Hertz+DMT
  reconstruction; keep every plot.

**Back-compatibility is the real constraint here.** Runs made before Phase 2
have no `contact_E_star`, and they must keep plotting. Every consumer takes the
stored column when present and falls back to the global estimate when it is
not, saying so on the figure — a silent fallback to a 750x-wrong number is what
this version is fixing.

## Verification

    python -m unittest discover -s tests -v
    python -m unittest tests.test_identity          # the one regression gate

New: `tests/test_stress.py`. The load-bearing cases are an analytic one
(a single contact between two granules on a known branch vector, where sigma is
exact by hand), the sign convention (a compressed pair gives P > 0), the 2D
trace (`/2`, not `/3`), twin parity, and the isotropy of a hydrostatically
compressed bed.

Phases 1 and 2 add keys and arrays; neither changes a trajectory, so
`tests/test_identity.py` must stay green **without re-blessing**. That is the
checkpoint that says the new physics is inert.

## Landed beyond the plan

**Stress-force-fabric and the small-strain flag** were added mid-version, after the
user pointed out that Love-Weber is usually quoted as a rigid-particle result and
asked about the CMN formulation and SFF. Both were worth doing and neither changed
the plan's shape:

* Love-Weber turns out to be **exact for this model** — the particle-centred form
  telescopes to the branch-vector form whatever the contact point, and CMN's extra
  terms are inertial and so identically zero overdamped. The real limit is the
  Hertz/JKR contact law underneath it, which is why the flag that shipped is
  `stress_patch_p95`, measured, rather than a correction term invented to look
  rigorous.
* SFF shipped as the upscaling, with `sff_closure` reported rather than absorbed
  because the tangential force is not on the contact record.

Verifying the SFF identity is what caught the **3x3-embedding deviator bug** — every
isotropic 2D state was reporting a shear of 0.577 P. That bug was in code written
earlier in this same version, and nothing else would have found it: the stress tests
that existed all passed.

## Still open (not tasks)

* **`a_t` is not on the contact record.** Adding a tangential-force column would let
  SFF close exactly instead of to 0.82–1.04. It is a kernel change in both twins, so
  it was not done on the way past.
* **`stress_patch_p95` = 0.22 on the 3D gravity bed.** Nothing is wrong with the
  stress; the contact law is being asked to work at the edge of its range. Worth
  knowing before quoting a 3D pressure.
* **Osmotic and interfacial have no energy scale.** `gamma = E_modulus *
  interface_width * 0.01` has no source. Giving them one is a modelling decision.
* **`P_vir` is the signal V3.6's convergence detector wanted.** It substituted
  `F_mean` for robotsim's `P_vir` because the engine had no pressure. It has one
  now, and a pressure is intensive where `F_mean` is not. Deliberately NOT
  changed here: the detector is off by default and its tolerances are explicitly
  uncalibrated, so swapping its signal mid-version would invalidate any
  calibration run against the old one. Worth doing with
  `pipeline/shadow_check.py --sweep`, as a change in its own right.
* **`gels/laguerre.py` tessellates on bounding spheres.** `radical_volumes` takes
  `gs.r` as the Laguerre weight while the numerator `gvol` uses the true shape
  volume from `shape_cache`, so for blocky granules `phi_loc_*` divides a real
  shape volume by a sphere-based territory. `viz2/voronoi.py` already has a
  shape-aware alternative -- its boundary-based "shrink-wrap" mode tessellates
  from points sampled on the granule SURFACE (`superellipse_polygon_pts`) and
  merges per granule. Found during the V3.7 sweep; not acted on, because this
  version's engine scope was the stress tensor.
* **A genuine intra-particle term would need deformation DOFs.** GELS granules
  are rigid bodies with a compliant contact law, so the elastic energy of
  deformation IS the JKR contact energy and there is no separate field to
  integrate. LS-DEM carried such DOFs and was assessed and retired in V3.2. A
  CMN-style second term is therefore a modelling commitment, not a
  post-processing change.
